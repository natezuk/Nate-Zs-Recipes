\title{Leek and mushroom tart}

\maketitle

\textcolor{green}{(11/6/2017)}

Based on the recipe in \textit{Baking \& Pastry}, p. 706.

\textbf{Ingredients:}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Leeks = 194 g
\item Scallions = 78 g \textcolor{red}{(was supposed to be shallots)}
\item Mushrooms = 221 g
\item Creme Fraiche = 8 oz \textcolor{red}{(didn't seem thick enough when using)}
\item Butter puff pastry (enough to cover pie pan)
\item Salt = 1/2 tsp
\item Pepper = 1/4 tsp
\end{enumerate}

Bake crust separately at 350 F for 15 min \textcolor{red}{(I used a pie pan for this instead of a tart pan)}.  Saute leeks and shallots in olive oil, then use the pan to saute mushrooms separately.  Combine leeks, shallots, and creme fraiche in a bowl and fill the pan with the crust.  Add mushrooms on top, then sprinkle on salt and pepper.  Bake at 375 F for 45 min.  Let cool for 20 min.

\textcolor{red}{I used the puff pastry dough that I had made previously.  I initially used enough to cover the pie pan and have a bit of crust. But when the pie baked the crust shrank and only went up the sides of the pan by 1 cm or so after baking for ~8.5 min. I then added the rest of the dough to fill in the crust on the walls and baked for another 10 min, and this seemed to do fine (it didn't really shrink further).}

\textcolor{red}{I baked the tart for 35 min before the oven started to smoke, probably because of juice from the tart that spilled over during cooking. After cooling for ~5 min the filling was not cooked enough; a knife after being stuck in the filling did not come out clean. I baked the tart for another 10 min and then let it cool for 20.  After baking the knife still didn't come out competely clean, but the crust was starting to burn.}

\textcolor{red}{The flavor of the tart is good, cheesy flavor, and could even use a bit more salt.  I actually don't like the puff pastry with this tart because it is too buttery. The creme fraiche I think was also too liquidy and may have needed to congeal more. If so, the filling might have ended up as the right consistency.}
