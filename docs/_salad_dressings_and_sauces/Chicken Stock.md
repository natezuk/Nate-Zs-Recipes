---
title: Chicken Stock
---
*I got this starting from a recipe from Forgotten Skills of Cooking and the Professional Chef, as well as some online recipes, but eventually settled on this recipe. The amounts of each veggie and herb is very flexible, I have made this without one or two of the veggies. What's key is the proportion of chicken to water, which is just under a 1:2 ratio. The resulting stock when cooled is gelatinous.*

This recipe makes about 4 L of chicken stock.

### Ingredients:

- 2.5 kg chicken scraps and bones
- About 2 Tbsp vegetable or canola oil
- 200-300 g onion, coarsely chopped, including the peels
- 200-300 g celery, coarsely chopped
- 200-300 g carrots, coarsely chopped, washed but not peeled
- 20-30 g garlic, base removed but don't chop or remove peels
- Stems of various herbs and veggies (optional, but don't use dill or cabbage)
- 4.5 L water
- 2 tsp salt
- 2 tsp black peppercorns
- 3 bay leaves

### Method:

1. Add the oil to a large pot and heat on medium. When the oil is heated, add the chicken scraps and bones, and mix regularly to fry the outside of the scraps.
2. Add the vegetables and mix in. Heat for a few minutes. Then add the stems if using and heat for another minute or so.
3. Add the water, then add the salt, peppercorns, and bay leaves. Cover the pot.
4. When the pot is boiling, turn to the lowest setting.
5. Boil covered for 2.5-3 hours. During the first couple of hours check on the stock every 20 minutes and use a spoon to remove scum and foam that forms on the top. At some point (about 2 hours), there won't be any more foam forming, and from this point the stock can be cooked for another 30 minutes at least.
6. Let cool for 15-30 minutes. Then use tongs to place the veggies and loose chicken pieces into a smaller bowl, and the peels, stems, and chicken bones in a big bowl. The veggies and loose chicken can be used for another dish, like curry, stir fry, or pot pie.
7. Using a large strainer, strain the stock into a metal pot that can withstand the heat of the stock. Sort the veggies, chicken, and scraps that have been strained. Let the stock cool, covered, for at least another hour until it is closer to room temperature.
8. Using a fine strainer, strain the stock into a large metal bowl. Cover the bowl, and place in the refrigerator to cool overnight. (The stock can be in the fridge for at most 2 days).
9. Pour the stock into smaller containers (*I use 0.5 or 1 L containers*) and put them in the freezer.
