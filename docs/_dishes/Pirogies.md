---
title: Pirogies
---
*Based on the recipe from [spend with pennies](https://www.spendwithpennies.com/homemade-pierogi/).*

Takes 2-3 hours, and makes about 40 pirogies.

### Ingredients:

#### *Dough*:
- 800 g plain flour
- 2 tsp salt
- 2 eggs
- 100 mL sunflower / canola oil
- 350 mL water

#### *Filling*:
- 1.5 kg potatoes
- 100 g butter
- 1 medium onion, finely diced

### Method:

*Making the dough*:
1. Combine the flour and salt in a large bowl. In a small bowl, beat the eggs, then add the oil and mix in. Add the egg and oil mixture to the large bowl with the flour and mix until fully combined.
2. Add the water to the flour mixture in two halves, mixing in after each addition. Once the water is fully incorporated, use your hands to knead the dough in the bowl. Knead for about a minute, until the dough is stretchy but not tight.
3. Wrap the dough in plastic wrap and set in the fridge for at least one hour.

*While dough is in the fridge, make the filling*:
4. Boil the potatoes in a large pot with salted water until the potatoes are tender. Strain the water, then mash the potatoes in a large bowl.
5. As the potatoes cool a bit, heat the butter in a small saucepan. Add the onion and cook until translucent. 
6. Let the butter and onions cool for about a minute before adding to the potatoes. Mix in until butter and onions evenly coat the mashed potatoes.

*Making the pirogies*:
7. Bring a large pot of water to a boil.
8. Starting with half of the dough, roll the dough out on a floured surface to about 1/4 cm thick, not much thicker than that. (*Note: the dough should be stretchy, even when rolled out quite thin. The dough may also be sticky after coming out of the fridge, so you might need a decent amount of flour for the surface.*)
9. Use a circular cutter (like a large glass) to cut the dough into 3-inch circles. (*You can stretch out the circle further at this point to make it thinner.*)
10. Add a large spoonful of filling to the center of the circle, then fold the dough in half around the filling and pitch the edges. (*Because the dough is stretchy, it can hold quite a bit of filling. Also, it's a bit sticky and can hold well at the edges.*). Set the pirogie aside on a surface or plate lightly dusted with flour, to keep the pirogie from sticking to the surface while sitting.
11. Repeat steps 8-10 until a batch of pirogies is made (8-10 pirogies) Place them in the pot of boiling water and boil for about 4 minutes, until the pirogies float. Remove and pirogies from the pot and place on a plate to cool.
12. Repeat steps 8-11 with the rest of the dough and filling.
*Once the pirogies have cooled, they can be frozen at this point.*

---
### Notes

*(19-4-2023)* I made this for the first time. The dough really needs to be thin, otherwise the pirogies have a pretty thick doughy shell. Because of this, it is useful to roll out the dough on a very large surface, or stretch out each circle before making the pirogie. They tasted great though!
